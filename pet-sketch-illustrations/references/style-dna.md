# Style DNA

## One Line

Pure white, minimal, hand-drawn ink pen sketch, spacious, restrained, weird, product-sketch feeling, Korean handwritten feel, structurally clear but not instructional.

Like an explainer sketch casually drawn with a black ink pen on white paper by someone who has spent years making AI, product, design, and developer tools.

## Must-Haves

- **16:9 horizontal** body illustration.
- **Pure white background (`#FFFFFF`)**: no beige, warm gray, paper texture, gradients, drop shadows, or retro paper feel.
- **Tactile black ink pen line art (NOT smooth vector)**:
  - Thin, slightly wobbly nib lines with double-sketched edges where appropriate.
  - **Ink hatching & micro-details**: parallel hatch lines on wood/stone/machines, stipple dots, short ground horizon lines, tiny grass tufts/pebbles, motion lines `( ( ) )`, and radial emphasis ticks.
  - **High-contrast ink weight**: solid black ink fills on key anchors (the black cat silhouette, dog's black ears/spot/nose, clips, machine rollers, dark shadows) so the illustration has visual weight and pops off white paper. Never draw characters or props purely as hollow uniform-stroke coloring-book outlines, and never draw background objects in faint gray (`#CCCCCC`) instead of crisp black ink.
- **Lots of empty space**: subject occupies 40%-60% of the frame, with at least 35%-45% clean blank white space.
- **Sparse Korean handwritten annotations**: 2-5 short Korean labels per image (1-3 words each; keep established tech terms like `API`, `RAG`, `Cache` when natural).
- **One core metaphor per image**: explain one cognitive move through a physical low-tech contraption, not a literal office diagram.

## 3-Color Narrative Grammar (Annotation & Flow Only)

Never color character bodies, cheeks, ears, or furniture surfaces (zero pastel tints, zero blush, zero colored pencil shading). Reserve color strictly for the 3-color narrative grammar:

- **Black**: primary ink line art, characters, contraptions, hatching, ground lines, main frames.
- **Orange**: main flow paths, dashed trajectories, directional arrows, cause-to-effect movement, outcome labels.
- **Red**: problems, friction, bottlenecks, stale/broken items (`X`), warnings, critical takeaways.
- **Blue**: specific operations/actions (`비우기`, `변환`), system states, metadata (`원본 변경`, `깊이`, `시간`), secondary checkmarks (`✔`).

Use all three roles when contrast helps explain tension (e.g., red problem vs. blue operation vs. orange flow). Keep color restrained.

## Absolute No

- No color tints, pastel blush, or body fills on animals or props.
- No hollow coloring-book outlines or smooth corporate vector icons.
- No literal modern office clipart (plain desk drawer, office filing cabinet, smooth cylinder office bin) when a weird physical contraption works better.
- No cute chibi mascot proportions, children's illustration, or commercial flat art.
- No PPT infographic, formal flowchart, course slide, or real software UI.
- No top-left titles like "Workflow", "System Architecture", or "캐시 구조".

## Aesthetic Direction

Weird, creative, deadpan, interesting, clean, and slightly unhinged.
The reader should first think, "That's a weirdly clever contraption," and understand the technical structure within one second.
